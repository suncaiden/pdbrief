Open with the finding in plain terms. Assume the reader knows nothing about the
topic and has no obligation to keep reading. Two or three short paragraphs.

:::key
The single most important thing to take away, in two or three sentences.
:::

## What the researchers were trying to find out

The question behind the study, and why anyone thought it was worth asking.

## What they did

Who was studied, how many, for how long, and what was measured. Enough detail
that a reader can judge the strength of the evidence for themselves.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Participants | 000 | 000 |
| Result | | |

## What they found

The results, in order of importance. Numbers where they help, plain description
where they do not.

## Why it matters

The consequence for patients, for researchers, or for how the field thinks.
Be specific and avoid overstating.

:::caution
Anything a reader might wrongly conclude, or any risk in acting on this.
:::

## What this doesn't tell us

The honest limits. Every issue has this section. Sample size, duration,
who was excluded, what was measured and what was not.

## What to watch next

The trials, questions, or follow-up work that will settle what this study left open.

:::plain
A closing paragraph a reader could repeat to someone else — what happened, and
what it does and does not change.
:::
